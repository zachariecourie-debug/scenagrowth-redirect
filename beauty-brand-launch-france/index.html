Beauty & fragrance launch in France:
the journey.

Bringing a fragrance brand to the French market takes methodical preparation. Here are the steps we suggest assessing to structure your project — an example path for a brand entering France, adapted to each brand, budget and level of maturity.

Discuss your launchUAE → France →
01 — The journeyPrepare · create desirability · control costs

Seven steps,assessed one by one.

A progressive method to prepare the brand’s arrival, build desirability and keep initial costs under control. This path is specific to a beauty or fragrance brand entering France; the UAE direction follows its own steps, channels and partners.

  • 01

    Analyse the market and positioning

    Category, competition, target customers, brand identity, prices and margins by channel — and the selection of the references (scents) to test with the intended customers.

  • 02

    Adapt the brand to the French market

    Branding, story, content, packaging and sales materials adapted to French expectations, while preserving the brand’s origin and heritage.

  • 03

    Prepare direct online sales

    Scoping Shopify or another suitable e-commerce channel and the providers it needs. In the launch path described here, the online shop is the only sales channel, to keep control of the customer experience and of distribution costs.

  • 04

    Organise scent discovery

    Sampling strategy, and temporary stands in selected shopping centres where the budget allows. They are for discovering and testing the scents only — no sales and no commercial stock on site. QR codes can lead visitors to the online shop.

  • 05

    Create the event

    A launch evening dedicated to the brand, with UGC content and influencer marketing. Scena can suggest relevant creator profiles; any ambassador depends on the project, the budget and the fit of the profile.

  • 06

    Assess urban visibility

    An outdoor campaign around the launch, as an option to be costed and measured.

  • 07

    Develop distribution after validation

    If results, margins and operational capacity allow: extending the ranges and prospecting concept stores, perfumeries, department stores and selective retailers. Private sales are assessed against the brand’s positioning.

Shopify, Paris events, creator collaborations, concept stores and shopping centres are options to assess according to the project, budget and results — not services included by default.

Options and resultsTransparency

Options studied,results not promised.

Events, creator collaborations, concept-store presence, mall stands, outdoor campaigns and other activations are studied according to each project’s needs and budget. A listing with a retailer or a chain depends on the buyer’s decision and on how the market receives the brand. It cannot be guaranteed in advance.

02 — Our missionsTest · enter · grow

Three missions,one decision at each step.

Depending on how advanced your project is, you can engage the whole journey or only the steps you need.

  • Mission 1 — Market AssessmentObjective: Assess whether your project is viable in the target market — France or the UAE.
    Scope: Category and competition, brand identity and price perception, customer expectations, sales channels and potential partners, indicative pricing and margins by channel.
    Decision: A clear recommendation: go ahead, adjust the project or wait.
  • Mission 2 — Market Entry PreparationObjective: Build the foundations of your brand in the target market.
    Scope: Positioning and messages adapted to the market, sales tools, channel choice, e-commerce and assortment scoping where relevant, product discovery and sampling strategy, budget, timeline and partner selection.
    Regulatory: An initial review of requirements and referral to qualified specialists — not a certification or a regulatory validation.
    Decision: Validation of the launch plan, budget and partners.
  • Mission 3 — Launch & Business DevelopmentObjective: Coordinate the agreed actions with the selected partners and grow the brand from the results.
    Scope: Business development and retail prospecting, partner coordination, brand activations (concept stores, content creators, events), import as importer of record, logistics with a partner and a partner distribution network — within the validated project and budget.
    Deliverable: Operational follow-up, regular reporting, adjustment of commercial actions and recommendations on next steps.
03 — Who does whatRoles · partners · commitments

Our role, our partnersand our commitments.

Scena leads and coordinates. Specialists and providers do what falls within their expertise — and you validate before anything is committed.

  • Scena Growth supportLeading the market-entry strategy, business development and overall coordination of the project.
  • Regulatory expertiseRegulatory obligations are checked by qualified specialists, under their own responsibility. Scena Growth can make a first review and refer the brand to them.
  • Operations & logisticsSCENA can handle import as importer of record, without buying your stock: it stays your property, with a partner logistician who stores and ships. Distribution to points of sale runs through a network of partner distributors that SCENA selects and coordinates. E-commerce and event production can also be entrusted to partner providers.
  • LimitsScena Growth does not guarantee listings, sales volumes or timelines that depend on buyers or third parties. Import, logistics and distribution terms are agreed in writing for each project.
  • Cost transparencyProviders’ responsibilities, prices and capacities are presented for validation before any commitment. The terms of Scena Growth’s mission are set out in writing before it starts.
04 — Regulation, in plain wordsCosmetics in the EU

Compliance comesbefore the shelf.

Cosmetics sold in the EU must meet the EU cosmetics regulation. In simple terms, obligations can include:

A Responsible Person established in the EUA Product Information File (PIF)A safety assessmentA CPNP notification before the product is placed on the marketLabelling rules, including information in French for France

References: European Commission — cosmetics legislation ↗ · ANSM — FAQ ↗

05 — Illustrative scenarioFictional example

Illustrative scenario:rolling out an international fragrance brand.

This example is fictional. It illustrates one possible method and the types of providers to assess. It is neither a client case nor a commitment to results.

  1. Project preparation, and referral to an independent specialist to check the applicable regulatory requirements.
  2. Positioning and pricing analysis, then assessment of a logistics provider suited to the need.
  3. Test of an e-commerce channel with a narrow assortment and a measurable sampling strategy.
  4. Assessment of an event or a temporary activation before widening B2B prospecting.
Questions

Straight
answers.

Do I need to take every step?

No. Each step is assessed; you can engage the whole journey or only the missions you need.

Does Scena handle regulatory compliance?

We make a first review and refer you to qualified specialists. Compliance remains the responsibility of the brand and its Responsible Person.

Do you guarantee listings or sales?

No. Listings depend on buyers’ decisions and on how the market receives the brand.

Do you hold stock or act as importer?

We do not take your stock: it stays your property, with a partner logistician who stores and ships. SCENA can, however, act as importer of record; distribution to points of sale runs through partner distributors. Terms are agreed per project before any commitment.
