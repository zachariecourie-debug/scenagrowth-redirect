Glossary
France ↔ UAE.

The words that come up when a perfume or beauty brand moves from one market to the other, defined simply, with sources for the regulatory terms. Guidance only, to be checked with a specialist before any decision.

01 — UAEDubai · Abu Dhabi

Selling inthe UAE.

UAE

Montaji (product registration in Dubai)

Dubai Municipality’s platform for registering products, including cosmetics and perfumes, before they are sold in Dubai. The municipality evaluates, registers, releases and monitors products on the market. The filing is made by a licensed local entity, usually the importer or the distributor.

Source: Dubai Municipality, role in cosmetics registration and Montaji+ (27/10/2025)

Preparing a Montaji file →

UAE

ECAS

The UAE conformity scheme run by the Ministry of Industry and Advanced Technology (MoIAT): it registers certain regulated products and issues a certificate confirming that they meet the approved specifications. Depending on the category, cosmetics and personal care products may be covered: to be checked with a specialist.

Source: MoIAT, conformity certificates for regulated products (ECAS)

UAE

GSO 1943

The GSO (Gulf Standardization Organization) publishes technical regulations shared by Gulf countries. GSO 1943 sets the safety, labelling and packaging requirements for cosmetics and personal care products; the current edition was approved in 2024.

Source: GSO 1943:2024, safety requirements of cosmetics and personal care products

UAE

Official importer (importer of record)

The company that clears the goods through customs in its own name and pays import duty and VAT. In the UAE, it needs a trade licence and a customs code. It can import for a brand without buying its stock, depending on the contract.

Sources: Meydan Free Zone, “Customs code in Dubai” (accessed 18/10/2026) · Dubai Customs

Selling in the UAE without a company →

UAE

Registered agent or distributor

In the UAE, Federal Law No. 3 of 2022 governs commercial agencies registered with the Ministry of Economy: territorial exclusivity and the end of the contract follow specific rules, to be read with an adviser before signing.

Source: CMS, “New dawn for commercial agencies in the UAE” (Federal Law 3/2022)

Finding a distributor in the UAE →

UAE

Mainland company

A company registered in an emirate outside the free zones, which can sell directly on the local market. Since the company law reform (Federal Decree-Law No. 32 of 2021), most activities can be fully foreign-owned; some remain restricted.

Source: UAE government portal (u.ae), full foreign ownership of commercial companies

Free zone or mainland? →

UAE

Free zone

An economic zone with its own authority, where a company can be fully foreign-owned. To sell on the local market, it works through a licensed mainland distributor, or opens a mainland branch or company.

Source: UAE government portal (u.ae), running a business in a free zone

Free zone or mainland? →

UAE

CIF value and customs duty

CIF (cost, insurance, freight): the value of the goods delivered to the port of arrival. In the UAE, customs duty is generally 5% of that value.

Source: Dubai Customs

The real price of a perfume in Dubai →

UAE

VAT in the UAE

A 5% VAT, charged at import and then on each sale (Federal Decree-Law No. 8 of 2017).

Source: Federal Tax Authority (Federal Decree-Law 8/2017)

UAE

Trademark registration in the UAE

Trademarks are registered with the federal Ministry of Economy and Tourism, online. Filing before appointing a distributor prevents a partner or a third party from registering it in its own name.

Source: UAE government portal (u.ae), intellectual property

02 — European UnionFrance · EU

Selling inFrance.

European Union

Responsible Person

The natural or legal person established in the EU, designated for each cosmetic placed on the EU market, who guarantees its compliance with Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009. For an imported product, it is the importer by default, unless another person is designated in writing.

Sources: Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 on cosmetic products, EUR-Lex · DGCCRF (French consumer authority), cosmetics guide

CPNP notification and the Responsible Person →

European Union

CPNP

The European Commission’s cosmetic product notification portal: the Responsible Person notifies each product there before it is placed on the market, with no further national notification within the EU.

Source: European Commission, Cosmetic Product Notification Portal (CPNP)

CPNP notification and the Responsible Person →

European Union

PIF (product information file)

The file kept available to the authorities for each cosmetic: product description, safety report, manufacturing method and proof of the claimed effects.

Source: Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 on cosmetic products, EUR-Lex

European Union

Fragrance allergens

Fragrance substances that must be named on the label above certain thresholds. Regulation (EU) 2023/1545 extends the list, with a phased application: dates to be checked in the official text.

Source: Regulation (EU) 2023/1545, EUR-Lex

Selling a Gulf perfume brand in France →

European Union

EORI number

The customs identification number of economic operators: required for any company established in the EU that imports or exports, and for a non-EU company that files customs declarations in the EU.

Source: European Commission, EORI number

Selling a Gulf perfume brand in France →

European Union

French-language labelling

In France, the mandatory information on a product sold to consumers must be in French (law of 4 August 1994).

Source: Law No. 94-665 of 4 August 1994, Légifrance

Price and sales

Price cascade

The path from the transfer price, the one the brand invoices, to the retail price on the shelf: transport, duties, the importer’s, distributor’s and retailer’s margins, then VAT. It is worked out from the shelf, not from the factory.
