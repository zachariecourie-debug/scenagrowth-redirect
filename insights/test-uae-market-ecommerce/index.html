Test the UAE market before you invest: the e-commerce model

A company, an office, a team, stock: setting up in Dubai is expensive before the first sale. For many French brands, the best first step is not to build but to test — online, fast, with real customers.

Scena InsightsUAE · France → UAE

Why test before you invest

The UAE is a highly connected market: almost the entire population is online, mobile shopping is second nature and consumers know European brands well. It is also a demanding market, where price, message and service must be adjusted. An e-commerce test checks all of this with real data, before committing tens of thousands of euros to a local structure.

What a test measures

  • 01Demand: do UAE visitors buy, and which of your products?
  • 02Price: at what level does the brand stay desirable once shipping, customs and VAT are included?
  • 03Message: which claims, visuals and creators trigger purchase in this market?
  • 04Channel: brand site, marketplaces such as Amazon.ae or Noon, social — what converts?
  • 05Customer: who actually buys — Emiratis, expatriates, French speakers — and what does acquiring them cost?

The building blocks of a test

A store, often Shopify, set up for the market: prices in dirhams (AED), content in English and, depending on the target, Arabic. Local payment methods: cards, Apple Pay and the buy-now-pay-later options widely used in the region, such as Tabby or Tamara. Fitting logistics: shipping from France to start with, or a small stock held by a fulfilment partner in Dubai for one- or two-day delivery. And visibility: digital campaigns, content and local creators.

What to watch

Testing does not mean ignoring the rules. Some categories — cosmetics, food, supplements — must be registered and labelled, often in Arabic, before they can be sold. Imports generally carry 5% customs duty and 5% VAT. And you need to decide from the start who is the importer, who is the seller (the “merchant of record”), and who handles returns and customer service. These choices depend on the product: settle them before launch, not after.

Reading the results

A serious test usually runs one to three months. Look at conversion rate, average order value, customer acquisition cost, repeat rate and returns — and at inbound messages: a distributor, concept store or hotel getting in touch is as valuable a signal as a sale.

And if it works?

The numbers become your best argument. They let you open a pop-up, convince a distributor or retailer, scale on a marketplace — and, if the market justifies it, set up a local structure knowing exactly why. If it does not work, you will have learned fast, for a fraction of the cost of a full setup.
