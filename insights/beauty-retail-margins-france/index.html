Understanding beauty retail margins in France

A beauty brand rarely fails in France because of its product. It fails when the price that works for the customer does not leave enough for each player in the chain. Understanding margins is the first test of a project.

Scena InsightsRetail

From public price to ex-works price

Work backwards from the price the customer will pay. Remove VAT, then the retailer’s margin, then — if there is one — the distributor’s or importer’s margin. What is left is the price the brand receives. Each step has to be viable for the player who takes it.

Each channel has its own economics

Direct online sales, concept stores, perfumeries, department stores and selective chains do not keep the same share, do not pay at the same time and do not ask for the same support. The right grid is built channel by channel, not with a single coefficient.

What weighs on top of margin

Testers and samples, point-of-sale materials, contributions to in-store activations or promotions, logistics, returns or unsold stock, and payment terms. They are part of the real cost of being on the shelf and should be modelled from the start.

Building your price grid

Start from the positioning and the public price that fits your brand, check the price perception against comparable brands, then test the sensitivity of your margin when a channel asks for more. Retailer terms are negotiated — your grid should show where your limits are.

Checklist

  • 01Target public price per channel
  • 02Margin left to each intermediary
  • 03Cost of testers, samples and materials
  • 04Logistics and payment terms
  • 05Your floor: the price below which you do not go
